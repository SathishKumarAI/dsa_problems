# Statistics resources — what to read, what to skip, and why

Curated 2026-09-12. Every URL below was opened and read, not taken from a search result; the ones
that could not be opened are quarantined in [Could not verify](#could-not-verify) and are not
recommended. 50 sources checked, 34 kept.

The reader this is built for already knows what a p-value is supposed to be and can write Python
(`src/data/flashcards.ts` is the calibration: CLT, Type I/II, Simpson's paradox, VIF, leakage).
So this is not an "intro to stats" dump. It is ordered for someone who wants the foundation
underneath those flashcards to be real rather than memorised, and who will end up owning A/B tests
and regression diagnostics for a living. The path through it is `SYLLABUS.md`; this file is the
reference.

## Start here — five, not forty

Everything below this section is reference. Read only these five, in this order, and you have the
whole subject at working depth.

| # | Resource | Why this one | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [StatQuest, *Statistics Fundamentals*](https://statquest.org/video_index.html) | 62 short videos, ordered basic to hard. The fastest route from "I can recite the definition" to "I can see the mechanism". | ~12 h |
| 2 | [Seeing Theory](https://seeing-theory.brown.edu/) | The only resource that makes **sampling distributions** physical. Fifteen minutes there replaces a chapter. | ~3 h |
| 3 | [*Introduction to Modern Statistics* 2e](https://openintro-ims.netlify.app/) | The one textbook. Simulation-first, free forever, solutions in the appendix. | ~30 h |
| 4 | [*Think Stats* 3e](https://allendowney.github.io/ThinkStats/) | Inference by writing the simulation instead of looking up the test. This is how you will actually work. | ~20 h |
| 5 | [MIT 18.05 problem sets + solutions](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-05-introduction-to-probability-and-statistics-spring-2022/) | Video and prose both produce false confidence. Graded problems with worked solutions are the only check. | ~25 h |

Roughly 90 hours. That is the honest number for "I know statistics" at working level.

## Books — free and legal online

| Book | Format / time | Level | Read these chapters | Skip on a first pass | Bad for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **[Introduction to Modern Statistics, 2e](https://openintro-ims.netlify.app/)** — Çetinkaya-Rundel & Hardin (OpenIntro). Free HTML always; [free PDF](https://www.openintro.org/book/ims/) by setting the price to $0. 32 R tutorials, labs, **solutions appendix**. | Book, ~30 h | None | Parts I–II (data, exploratory analysis), then IV–V (foundations of inference, statistical inference) — that is the core. | [OpenIntro Statistics, 4e](https://www.openintro.org/book/os/) — 9 chapters, free PDF | Book, ~25 h | None | — | — | **Drop it.** The predecessor of IMS, covering the same ground more traditionally. Keeping both is pure duplication. Worth it only if you specifically want formula-first inference before simulation-first. |
| **[Think Stats, 3e](https://allendowney.github.io/ThinkStats/)** — Allen B. Downey (Green Tea Press). 14 chapters, CC-licensed, every chapter a runnable Jupyter notebook with Colab links. | Book + notebooks, ~20 h | Basic Python | Ch. 1–6 (exploratory analysis, distributions, PMF, CDF, modelling distributions, PDF), **ch. 8–9 (estimation, hypothesis testing — the heart of the book)**, ch. 10–11 (least squares, multiple regression). | Ch. 12–13 (time series, survival) unless you need them; ch. 14 (analytic methods) is an appendix wearing a chapter number. | Theory. | [*Introduction to Statistical Learning*, Python edition (ISLP)](https://www.statlearning.com/) — James, Witten, Hastie, Tibshirani, Taylor. Free PDF linked from the site (served via the authors' Drive). | [*Regression and Other Stories*](https://users.aalto.fi/~ave/ROS.pdf) — Gelman, Hill, Vehtari. Unmatched on interpretation, uncertainty and workflow. | The Stan / `rstanarm` machinery early on, if you are not going Bayesian. | Python users (R throughout) and anyone wanting recipes — it argues with you instead. Large. Come back after regression has bitten you once. |
| [*Statistics Done Wrong*](https://www.statisticsdonewrong.com/) — Alex Reinhart. Full text free (CC-BY 4.0); expanded print edition from No Starch. | Book, ~3 h | Knows what a p-value is | **Every trap in one sitting**: power, pseudoreplication, p-value misinterpretation, researcher degrees of freedom, regression to the mean. The highest value per hour on this list. | Nothing. Read all of it. | Learning to do anything — it says what goes wrong and never teaches the method that goes right. Read it *after* the inference chapters, never before. |
| [*Computer Age Statistical Inference*](https://hastie.su.domains/CASI/) — Efron & Hastie (Stanford). Free PDF, personal use only. | Book, ~40 h | Graduate | The arc from Fisher to the bootstrap to machine learning — why the field carries three incompatible philosophies. | All of it, on a first pass. | Beginners, completely. Listed so it is not rediscovered later as a beginner recommendation. |

## Python

One tutorial each. The rest of every project's docs is a reference manual you grep, not a document you read.

| Library | The one thing to read | Time | Use it for | Bad for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **scipy.stats** | [The official statistics tutorial](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/tutorial/stats.html) — continuous and discrete distributions, the frozen-RV pattern, KDE, quasi-Monte Carlo, hypothesis tests, resampling | ~2 h | Distributions, one-off tests, `bootstrap` and `permutation_test`. The default. | The docs themselves say "work in progress" and "many, but certainly not all" features — coverage is genuinely uneven. Output is bare: a statistic and a p-value, no effect size, no CI, no degrees of freedom. And the `loc`/`scale` parameterisation will not match your textbook's, so keep [Distribution Explorer](https://distribution-explorer.github.io/) open beside it. |
| **statsmodels** | [Getting started](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/gettingstarted.html) — one OLS worked end to end on the Guerry data, ~30 min — then the [examples gallery](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/examples/index.html): 12 categories of runnable notebooks, 9 on linear regression alone | ~4 h | Regression with **inference**: standard errors, confidence intervals, t and F tests, `summary()`, diagnostics, GLMs, and `multipletests` for Bonferroni/FDR. Exactly what scikit-learn deliberately withholds. | Discoverability. Two parallel interfaces (`sm` arrays and `smf` R-style formulas) and the docs never firmly recommend one. The time-series half (16 + 15 examples) is a different, much heavier product wearing the same name. Error messages are poor. |
| **pingouin** ([v0.6.1](https://pingouin-stats.org/)) | [Guidelines](https://pingouin-stats.org/guidelines.html) — which-test-to-use flowcharts for ANOVA, correlation and non-parametric tests, with assumption checks shown *before* the test in every sample | ~1 h | One call, complete answer: `pg.ttest` returns T, p, **df, Cohen's d, the 95% CI of the difference, power and BF10**. The assumption-first flowcharts are a teaching artefact as much as a library. | Trusting it blindly. Its own docs say "provided with NO WARRANTY", "still under heavy development and there are likely hidden bugs. Always double check the results with another statistical software." Take that literally. Pandas-centric, and far narrower than statsmodels. |
| **Cross-cutting** | [Common statistical tests are linear models](https://lindeloev.github.io/tests-as-linear/) — Jonas Kristoffer Lindeløv, ~8000 words | ~2 h | The highest-leverage idea in applied statistics: t-tests, correlation, ANOVA, ANCOVA, Wilcoxon, Mann-Whitney, Kruskal-Wallis and chi-square are all `y = β₀ + β₁x + …`. Collapses a memorised table of tests into one model you already know. | Being in R — a Python port is referenced but is not the canonical document — and being last updated in 2019. The rank-based equivalences are approximations and the page says so; read that caveat rather than skipping it. |

Not recommended: the [Statistics with Python specialization](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/statistics-with-python) (Michigan, 3 courses, ~57 h, numpy/pandas/statsmodels/seaborn) is **not free to audit** — Coursera offers financial aid instead. Think Stats covers the same ground for free and teaches better habits.

## Overlaps — what to drop

Two resources teaching the same thing is not redundancy insurance, it is a tax.

| Overlap | Keep | Drop | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| OpenIntro Statistics 4e vs **Introduction to Modern Statistics 2e** | IMS | OS4 | Same lineage, same material; IMS is the successor and is simulation-first. |
| ISLR (R, 1st/2nd ed) vs **ISLP** (Python) | ISLP | ISLR | Identical text. Take the one in your language. |
| Khan Academy vs CMU OLI | Khan | OLI | Same curriculum, same level; Khan has better UX and no Minitab. Take OLI only if you specifically want StatTutor's feedback. |
| StatQuest vs Khan videos | Both, for different jobs | — | StatQuest to *understand*, Khan to *drill*. Do not watch both sets of explanations of the same topic. |
| **Statistics Done Wrong** (3 h) vs Lakens (30 h) vs Calling Bullshit (10 h) | Statistics Done Wrong | The other two, initially | All three teach how inference goes wrong. Reinhart is a tenth of the time for most of the value. Add Lakens only once you are designing experiments. |
| Seeing Theory vs R Psychologist | Both | — | Genuinely different jobs: Seeing Theory is a course-shaped tour, R Psychologist is a deep dive on power and intervals. |
| Think Stats vs MIT 18.05 | Both | — | Exact mirror images: simulation with no theory, theory with no simulation. Together they are complete; alone each has a hole. |
