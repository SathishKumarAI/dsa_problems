## 0. Two numbers this repo was quoting that are wrong

Both were quoted in PRs merged earlier the same day. Corrected here rather than quietly.

**"Prose is ~90 characters a line" — it is 108.** Three gates compute characters as
`width ÷ (fontSize × 0.5)`. Manrope's real average glyph is **0.447em**, so every one of them
under-reports by about eleven characters. Counted glyph by glyph on rendered lines: median full line
**95–97** at 1600 and 1920, widest **108**, and only **28 of 96** wrapping blocks land inside the
45–75 band. The gates report 90, assert ≤ 96, and pass.

**The reading room's 36px was blamed on the wrong element.** `DESIGN.md §The chrome stays put`
deleted the hide-on-scroll chrome because it bought 36px and rewrapped every paragraph. Measured
properly at 1600, by hiding each piece and re-reading every paragraph:

| state       | content column | capped paragraph                        | `scrollHeight` |
| ----------- | -------------- | --------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| today       | 970            | left 306, width 816                     | 17 432         |
| rail hidden | 1250           | **left 306, width 816 — identical**     | 17 232         |
| both hidden | 1590           | **left 34** (−272px), width 816         | 17 156         |

The measure is **816px inside a 970px column** — 154px of slack — so the rail leaving moves nothing
that is capped. The 36px _and_ the rewrap came from the column swapping **its own cap**
(`max-w-reading → max-w-page`). What actually hurt is the **left sidebar**: its exit moves every
paragraph's left edge **272px**, a position change on the line being read. That distinction is what
makes the gesture buildable again (§5).

## 4. What the gates cannot see

Every finding above sits on a page whose suite is green. These three are what the green was covering
for.

1. **The measure gates estimate characters instead of counting them.** `width ÷ (fontSize × 0.5)`
   against a real 0.447em average glyph: they report 90 where the page renders 108, then assert
   ≤ 96. Fix: measure a rendered line's real text (canvas `measureText`, or `getClientRects()` per
   line divided by the text's own length) and print the number.
2. **The motion gate reads transitions only, and never visits a problem page.** It checks
   `transitionTimingFunction` — never `animationTimingFunction` — and walks home, the journey and
   the visualizer. All 13 off-spec elements are on the page it does not open.
3. **The page-debt ledger counts authored FIELDS, not behaviours.** This page owes **0** by the
   repo's own measure, and all nine teaching gaps in §6 are invisible to it. A page can be complete
   by its own ledger and still never ask the reader a question after the first 5% of it.

## 5. The chrome, on scroll — the spec that answers the owner's ask

The owner asked for the hide-on-scroll gesture back the same day it was deleted. §0 shows why that
is now buildable without the cost, and what the gain actually is.

| #       | rule                                                                                                                    | number                                                      |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| S1      | Both rails leave: the inner column takes `translateX(±12px)` + `opacity 0` + `inert`                                      | 320ms, house curve                                            |
| S2      | **The gate.** Every paragraph's `left` and `width`, and `document.scrollHeight`, identical hidden vs shown                | ±0                                                            |
| S3      | **Both tracks stay reserved** (the rail's `w-56`, the sidebar's 256) so the column never grows                            | ±0px of reflow                                                |
| S4      | Therefore no feedback loop: nothing shortens, so the browser cannot clamp `scrollY` and fake an upward gesture            | attempt #4's bug, structurally impossible                     |
| S5      | Hysteresis: hide on **72px** cumulative down, restore on **48px** up, accumulator reset on every turn                     | ≈ 4 lines; the first attempt flipped on 8px                   |
| S6      | Top zone **entered, not occupied** — restore on crossing 240 upward, never hide below it                                  | 240 = bar + first band                                        |
| S7      | Settle window after each flip                                                                                             | 200ms                                                         |
| S8      | Guards: focus inside the rail · an open dialog · a `scrollToId` correction in flight · reduced motion (does not hide)      | 4                                                             |
| S9      | Explicit wins: once `f` or the toggle is pressed, scroll never touches that rail again this session                       | `pageRail` is authoritative                                   |
| **S10** | **The orient bar collapses to one row on the same gesture** — it is out of flow, so this costs no reflow at all           | **105px → 51px: +54px of viewport**, against the rails' 0% of the measure |
| **S11** | The bar publishes its live height as `--orient-h`, and `html { scroll-padding-top: var(--orient-h) }` reads it everywhere | fixes C3 / L1 / L2 in the same commit                         |

**If the column is ever allowed to take the freed width**, the uncapped two-column grid cells must
take `max-w-measure` first — they measured 442 → 583px with their left edges moving 815 → 955.
Capped prose does not move; those cells do.

### What the outside says, and where it was checked

- **Brown interactive Rust Book** (Crichton et al., OOPSLA 2024, `arxiv.org/abs/2401.01257`):
  62 526 readers, 1 140 202 answers, 12 prose interventions, **+20% measured** on the targeted
  questions. The mechanism is the _authored wrong answer_, not the quiz.
- **Stripe's API reference**: the example is pinned beside the prose, so the text never says "see
  above" and "above" is never four screens away (split-attention — Tarmizi & Sweller, 1988).
- **Ciechanowski**: the figure sits under the sentence it proves, and it is draggable, not a video.
- **Distill**: a symbol in the prose and the same symbol in the figure share a hover.
- **Comeau**: at most three controls per widget, and the default is already the interesting case.
- **MDN**: identical section order on tens of thousands of pages — you learn by returning cheaply.
- **Generation effect** (Slamecka & Graf, 1978) and **retrieval practice** (Roediger & Karpicke,
  2006) are what items 1–3 are buying.
- **Contested, and left contested:** line length. Dyson & Haselgrove measured worse comprehension at
  100 characters than at 55; the owner has twice chosen "prose fills the column". §7 asks.
